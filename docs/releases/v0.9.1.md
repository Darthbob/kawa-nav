The first ride recorded by a rider on a real bike settled two things the documentation had only guessed at. How KawaNav talks to the bike is unchanged from 0.9.

### Changed
- **The IMU's three accelerations now say what they measure.** The sensor is mounted at 45° on a Z900: at a standstill gravity is split evenly between two of its channels, so what the screen used to show as "forward acceleration" was mostly gravity (a braking second read −1.4 g). Turning the pair by 45° gives 0.00 g forward and 1.00 g down at a standstill, and on the move it follows the acceleration the bike reports itself. The screen, the chart and the GPX now show forward / up / sideways, computed from the same recorded columns (so old rides are fixed too, without a re-record); the three raw channels stay on the debug card.
- **The traction-control readings are described for what they are.** 0x4A byte 24 and 0x4B byte 51 hold two *different* pairs that Rideology calls by the same name (`tcs_level_hb`/`tcs_level_lb`), and Kawasaki's app never interprets either of them. On the recorded ride the 0x4B pair moved exactly in the second the rear wheel spun up (4.6 km/h faster than the front) while the 0x4A nibbles stayed at zero all ride. The live row no longer claims "working: yes/no" but shows the raw nibbles marked unverified, and the ride summary counts either pair as the traction control having worked.
- **The high nibble of 0x4A byte 24 is recorded too** (column `tcs_hi`): it was decoded and then dropped, so half of that reading was invisible in the ride file.

### Documented
- `docs/PROTOCOL.md` gained what a real Z900 actually sends, field by field: the IMU block present but the suspension block absent (no electronic suspension), never any tyre pressure, brake pressure, torque, outside temperature or fuel rate, oil temperature once in a 24-minute ride, instant fuel in 80 % of the seconds. Values a bike does not send are absent, not "unverified".
- The two pairs of traction-control values, their naming collision, Rideology's own inconsistency about which half is "hb", and the two cheap tests that would settle what they mean: change KTRC between two rides, and provoke a deliberate cut-in with the dash lamp in view.

### Not yet tried on a bike
- **What the two TCS pairs report** (the rider's level, an intervention, or something else), and which of the three "rotation rates" is which axis: one of them follows the lean angle, one its rate of change, the third neither. Both need the tests above.
- Unchanged from 0.8/0.9: the dashboard's capability answer, writing riding modes 5-7 (debug build), the byte 88 wheel-speed setting, and the Waze test while moving.

### Which APK
- **`KawaNav-v0.9.1.apk`**: the app for everyday use.
- **`KawaNav-v0.9.1-debug.apk`**: the same app with the test tools and the guarded writing of ride settings and shift lamp; tap the Settings title seven times. Both are signed with the same key, so one installs over the other and back, keeping rides, refuels, battery readings, the maintenance log and settings.

### Install / update
Open the APK on the phone (Android 13+). It updates 0.9 in place (and anything from v0.4 on) and keeps everything those versions saved. Known limitations: [README](https://github.com/jackyes/kawa-nav/blob/v0.9.1/README.md#-known-limitations).
