A tool for testers: the debug build lists what is still to be tried at the bike, and works out two checks of the bike's health from the recorded rides. The normal app and the bike's protocol are unchanged from 1.2.3.

### For testers (debug build)

`KawaNav-v1.2.4-debug.apk` has a new screen in Settings → (tap the title 7 times) → "Bike data" → **Field tests**:

- **What is still to be tried, one card per test**: why it matters, the steps, and what counts as an answer, with a mark for the ones done. Thirteen tests in four groups: bike health, what KawaNav writes to the bike (ride settings, shift lamp, the profile name), what the recorded values mean (the traction control and ABS values, the first IMU rotation rate, the values against the dash) and the protocol (undecoded bytes, the 0x08 init, the notification categories). They are the open questions of [docs/PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/Darthbob/kawa-nav/blob/v1.2.4/docs/PROTOCOL.md).
- **Rear tyre and the wheel speeds.** The Z900 has no tyre pressure sensors, but it sends both wheel speeds. On steady, upright riding the rear wheel turns 0.988 times as fast as the front, the same to 0.06% from one ride to the next (six rides, 290 km). The ratio follows the tyres' rolling radius, so a soft rear tyre should pull it down, the way cars without pressure sensors find a slow puncture. The screen gives it per ride and speed band, next to the usual value. The test: one ride at the usual pressure and one with the rear 0.5 bar lower. If the low ride does not read clearly lower, the warning is not worth building.
- **Clutch and the gearing.** In a gear, engine speed per km/h of the rear wheel is fixed while the clutch holds (107.2 / 80.6 / 66.2 / 57.5 / 50.4 / 44.2 on the Z900, 1st to 6th). The screen compares the seconds at full throttle in 3rd gear and up with the same gear at light throttle: a slipping clutch would read above it. On the six rides they read 1–4% below it, never more than 1.1% above.

Nothing on this screen warns the rider: it shows the numbers, so that the tests can say whether a warning would work.

### Not yet tried on a bike

The two checks were run on six recorded Z900 rides (they agree with a separate analysis of the exported files) and are covered by unit tests (`WheelChecksTest`, `FieldTestsTest`); the screen was opened on an emulator with two of those rides. The tyre test itself, with the rear tyre let down, has not been done yet.
